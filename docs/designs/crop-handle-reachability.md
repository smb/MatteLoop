# Design: Reachable crop handles

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-09-03, reviewed the same day
Branch: chore/crop-handle-design
Repo: smb/MatteLoop
Status: REVIEWED (second pass 2026-09-03: priority, focus, thin crops, clamp plumbing settled)

## Problem Statement

The crop editor works, but its handles are hard to grab. Two independent causes,
both in the geometry layer:

**1. The frame is fitted flush to the widget border, so there is no room for a
handle.** `MediaTransform.scale` (`src/matteloop/core/geometry.py:311`) is
`min(viewport.width / display.width, viewport.height / display.height)`. On the
constrained axis the content rectangle touches the widget edge exactly. With the
default crop (the whole frame) the handle centres therefore land *on* that edge:
the 8x8 markers built at `geometry.py:586` are half-clipped, and
`_effective_target` (`geometry.py:928`) clamps the 24 px pointer region wholly
inward. Approaching the edge from outside hits the `PreviewStage` layout margin,
which is not a canvas at all.

**2. Between the handles, the border is not a drag target.** Each handle owns
one 24x24 box. A left edge has three of them (two corners plus `west`), so on a
225 px edge roughly 72 px resize and the remaining ~150 px fall through to the
`"crop"` entry in `priority` (`geometry.py:613`) and translate the whole
rectangle instead.

## What already exists

- `_TimelineTransform.inset` (field at `geometry.py:625`, clamped at `:636`) —
  an inset field with a build-time clamp already exists on the sibling
  transform in this file. `MediaTransform.inset` follows its shape rather than
  inventing one. The sibling clamps against width only; `MediaTransform.inset`
  must clamp against both axes.
- `_effective_target(rect, viewport, minimum)` (`geometry.py:928`) already grows
  a rectangle to a minimum size and clamps it into the viewport. The edge bands
  reuse it; they must not be built directly (see Constraints).
- **`tests/ui/test_crop_canvas.py:131-141` already guards the agreement between
  what Qt paints and what the geometry computes.** It pins the pixmap size on
  both axes and derives x and y from `(canvas.width() - pixmap.width()) / 2`,
  i.e. it assumes `AlignCenter` (`preview_canvas.py:136`) and symmetric
  margins, rather than observing the painted rectangle directly. Step 3
  therefore has to keep the four layout margins equal. This matters for the
  approach below: the two computations are guarded, just not by direct
  observation of where Qt painted.

## Constraints

- `core/geometry.py` is Qt-free and deterministic. The geometry belongs there,
  not in event handlers.
- **The edge bands must be produced through `_effective_target`.** Building them
  directly skips its viewport clamp and breaks
  `test_hit_targets_use_the_full_viewport_when_it_is_below_minimum_size`
  (`tests/core/test_geometry.py:153`), which asserts every target collapses to
  the whole viewport when the widget is smaller than one target.
- `core/geometry.py` is frozen at 1057 lines in `scripts/guardrails-baseline.json`.
  Growth was agreed with the maintainer during review, which is the order
  `docs/engineering-guardrails.md:288` requires.
- Verification: `uv run ruff check . && uv run mypy src &&
  QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen uv run pytest -q`

## Premises

1. The reported symptom is the crop drag surface, not the file drop zone.
   Drag-and-drop lives in `ui/source_strip.py:95`; `CropCanvas` accepts no drops.
2. Reachability does not depend on the exact gutter width. Measured against
   `_effective_target` at viewport 100: a 16 px gutter leaves 12 clickable
   pixels outside the image, an 8 px gutter leaves 8. At 8 the band covers the
   whole gutter; at 16 the outermost 4 px of the gutter are dead (the band is
   [4, 28]). An earlier draft asserted
   `_MEDIA_INSET >= _POINTER_TARGET / 2`; that relationship is not what carries
   the fix, and the runtime clamp violates it on small viewports while a
   constant-comparison test would stay green. Test reachability, not the ratio.

## Recommended Approach

### 1. One clamp, read once, plumbed to Qt after the fact

`MediaTransform` gains an `inset`. `CropCanvas` passes `_MEDIA_INSET` to the
geometry only, as the constructor argument to `MediaTransform`. It does not
also pass `_MEDIA_INSET` to the layout: after `_rebuild_geometry` runs, it
reads back `self._geometry.transform.inset` — the clamped value, per §2 — and
sets the layout contents margins from that, then calls `_update_pixmap`.
`_update_pixmap` (`preview_canvas.py:202-209`) shrinks the scaling box by
whatever the layout margins are and Qt centres the result, so this ordering
is what makes Qt paint into the rectangle `content_rect` predicts at every
size — passing the raw constant to both is only "one number read twice"
while the §2 clamp never fires. At a 20x10 viewport the clamp brings the
transform's inset down to 2.5 px while `_update_pixmap` would still subtract
16 px per side and scale to 1x1 if the layout kept the raw constant; the two
fits would diverge exactly where the clamp exists to be tested.

`test_crop_canvas.py:131-141` asserts the two results match. That test also
discharges the zoom risk: `MediaTransform` carries `zoom` and `pan` fields that
nothing sets today, and the day someone sets them Qt will not scale the pixmap
to match — so the test goes red at the moment the assumption breaks, which is
the right moment.

An earlier draft had `CropCanvas` paint the frame itself so that one snapshot
placed everything. It was dropped: it removes a working guard in order to
rebuild the property that guard already provides, and it drags in a scaled
pixmap cache, a paint-order constraint against the crop guard, and device pixel
ratio invalidation across monitors — three failure modes that do not otherwise
exist.

### 2. `MediaTransform.inset`, clamped where the sibling clamps

```python
inset = min(self.inset, self.viewport.width / 4, self.viewport.height / 4)
object.__setattr__(self, "inset", inset)
```

`inset` goes through `_finite` and rejects a negative value with `ValueError`,
matching the validation `pixel_aspect`, `zoom` and `dpr` already get
(`geometry.py:276-283`).

`scale` then fits into `viewport - 2 * inset` on both axes, and `content_rect`
stays centred in the full viewport so every widget coordinate remains
widget-absolute.

`inset` defaults to `0.0` and only the crop display geometry passes a non-zero
value. This is not cosmetic: `crop.py:139` builds a `MediaTransform` as a
unit-scale orientation transform whose viewport equals the display size exactly.
A non-zero default would shrink that fit and corrupt every oriented-coordinate
conversion.

The clamp is not decorative either: `crop_canvas.py:274` builds the viewport as
`SizeF(max(1, self.width()), max(1, self.height()))`, and those `max(1, ...)`
exist because Qt hands the widget through at zero width during layout. Without
the clamp the fit goes negative and `RectF` raises inside `content_rect`
(`geometry.py:82`), `_rebuild_geometry` swallows it into
`self._geometry = None`, and the overlay silently disappears until the next
resize.

### 3. Full-length edge bands

In `build_crop_geometry`, the pointer and touch rectangles for `north`, `south`,
`east` and `west` span the whole crop edge instead of a square — west covers
`x ∈ [left - 12, left + 12]`, `y ∈ [top, bottom]`. They are produced through
`_effective_target`, per Constraints. `visual` is untouched: the markers stay
8x8; only what counts as a hit changes.

The corners do not keep winning for free. `_CROP_HANDLES`
(`geometry.py:24-33`) is clockwise — `north_west, north, north_east, east,
south_east, south, south_west, west` — so `north` precedes `north_east`,
`east` precedes `south_east`, `south` precedes `south_west`. With full-length
bands and that order as `priority`, three of the four corners would lose the
half of their square that lies on the adjacent edge: on crop `(20, 20, 60,
60)` (`x, y, width, height`; corners at `(20,20)`–`(80,80)`), the point
`(74, 20)` sits inside both the north band (`y ∈ [8, 32]` across the whole
top edge) and the `north_east` corner square (`x ∈ [68, 92]`, `y ∈ [8, 32]`),
and `north` answers first because it comes first in `_CROP_HANDLES`.

`build_crop_geometry` therefore passes an explicit priority instead of
`(*_CROP_HANDLES, "crop")`: the four corners, then `"crop"`, then the four
edges — `north_west, north_east, south_east, south_west, crop, north, east,
south, west`. `_CROP_HANDLES` itself is unchanged; it is only consumed at
`geometry.py:613` today, and its clockwise order is still the natural focus
and visual order, just not the hit-test priority.

The pointer and touch rectangles for `"crop"` are the crop rectangle shrunk
inward by half the band thickness (12 px pointer, 22 px touch) on every side,
but only when the shrunken rectangle still measures at least the target
minimum (24 / 44) on both axes; otherwise the full crop rectangle is used.
Both still go through `_effective_target`. `visual["crop"]` and
`focus["crop"]` are unchanged — only the hit rectangles shrink.

Effect: on a large crop the interior moves, the 12 px border resizes, and the
corners win their squares. On a thin crop (below 48 px on an axis) the whole
interior still moves, and the bands are grabbable from outside the crop edge.
Because the focused target uses the same rectangle (`hit_test`,
`geometry.py:549`, promotes `focused` before `priority`, and
`CropCanvas._focused_target` starts as `"crop"`, `crop_canvas.py:55`), a
focused region no longer steals the inner half of any band or corner.


### 4. The four sizes get names

`_HANDLE_MARKER = 8.0`, `_POINTER_TARGET = 24.0`, `_TOUCH_TARGET = 44.0`,
`_MEDIA_INSET = 16.0` as module constants beside `_CROP_HANDLES`. The guard
against someone trimming the gutter is the reachability test below, not an
inequality between two of the constants — see Premise 2.

## NOT in scope

- **Painting the frame from the geometry snapshot** — see §1. Worth revisiting
  the day zoom or pan is wired up, and `test_crop_canvas.py:131-141` will say
  when.
- **Device pixel ratio on the previews** — `_update_pixmap` scales to logical
  points and never calls `setDevicePixelRatio`, so both previews are soft on
  Retina while `StatusLabel` (`preview_canvas.py:95`) already does it right for
  icons. Real, visible, and unrelated to crop handles. TODO candidate.
- **A resize cursor or hover affordance on the edge bands** — full-length bands
  change what a click on the middle of an edge does, with no visible signal.
  TODO candidate.
- **Handles offset outward from the crop edge (Figma style)** — cosmetic; needs
  the focus-ring path reworked.
- **Wiring up zoom and pan** — the fields exist; nothing here exposes them.
- **A focused edge beating its two corners on the overlap strip after a resize** — the focused target is promoted before priority; a Tab or a corner drag restores the corner.

## Failure modes

| Path | Failure | Test | Handling | Visible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `MediaTransform.__post_init__` | viewport below `2 * inset` | new | clamp (§2) | would be silent |
| `MediaTransform` in `crop.py:139` | a non-zero default inset | new | default `0.0` (§2) | would be silent |
| `build_crop_geometry` | band escapes the viewport | `test_geometry.py:153` | `_effective_target` | would be silent |
| paint vs geometry | the two fits disagree | `test_crop_canvas.py:131-141` | existing guard | would be silent |
| `MediaTransform.__post_init__` | negative or NaN inset | `test_geometry.py:379-396` gains two cases | `_finite` + sign check | would be silent |
| `hit_test` | corner click resolves to an edge band | new corner test | explicit corners-first priority | would be silent |
| `hit_test` | focused region hides the bands' inner half | new focused test | region rect shrunk by the band half-thickness | would be silent |
| `hit_test` | thin crop cannot be moved by mouse | new thin-crop test | full crop rect below 48 px | would be silent |
| resize order | margins set from the stale transform | new 20x10 test | geometry before margins before pixmap | one-frame drift |

No critical gap: every silent failure above is closed by a clamp or a default,
and each is paired with a test.

## Test plan

Three existing UI tests change; one of them changes in kind, not in number:

- `tests/ui/test_crop_canvas.py:131` pins `transform.scale` to `478 / 1280`;
  with a 16 px inset it becomes `446 / 1280`.
- `test_crop_canvas_drag_uses_the_fitted_frame_scale` (`:146`) does not pin the
  scale; it pins the drag result `CropSpec(314, 100, 400, 200)` at `:167`. Its
  press at `QPoint(80, 200)` sits 3.6 px below the crop's top edge under the
  inset (10 px today) and lands inside the new full-length north band. The
  horizontal move then has no vertical delta, `_resize_crop` leaves the crop
  unchanged, `_emit_drag` (`crop_canvas.py:243`) emits nothing, and
  `events[-1]` raises IndexError. The press and release points must move into
  the interior, clear of the 12 px bands (for example y = 230), and the
  expected `CropSpec` is recomputed from the inset scale.
- `test_crop_canvas_drag_emits_oriented_crop_changes_from_shared_geometry`
  (`:38`) uses the 200x180 minimum-size canvas (`preview_canvas.py:137-138`).
  Its scale drops from 2.0 to 1.68, so the 20x10 widget drag rounds to 12x6
  source pixels and `CropSpec(20, 15, 40, 20)` at `:50` becomes
  `CropSpec(22, 16, 40, 20)`.

The core geometry tests are unaffected: `test_geometry.py:66`, `:111`, `:167`
and `test_crop.py:58` build their `CropGeometryState` without an inset, and the
field defaults to `0.0`.

New tests:

1. `test_geometry.py` — `content_rect` is inset on both axes.
2. `test_geometry.py` — parametrised over viewports (1,1), (400,1) and (1,400):
   `inset == min(16, w/4, h/4)`, `scale > 0`, and the geometry still builds. A
   width-only copy of the sibling clamp passes at 1x1 and fails at 400x1.
3. `test_geometry.py` — a `MediaTransform` built without an explicit inset
   reports `inset == 0.0`; the coordinates are already pinned by
   `test_geometry.py:31-63` and `test_crop.py:58`.
4. `test_geometry.py` — built with `focused=None`, parametrised over gutters
   8, 16 and 24 (0 is excluded: there the point outside the content rectangle
   is outside the viewport, which is the defect): a point just outside the
   content rectangle hit-tests to the adjacent edge handle.
5. `test_geometry.py` — built with `focused=None`, a point a quarter of the
   way down the left edge hit-tests to `west`, not `crop`, asserted on both
   halves of the band (`left - 6` and `left + 6`).
6. `test_crop_canvas.py` — a drag started outside the frame emits `CropChanged`.
7. `test_geometry.py` — parametrised over the four corners: built with
   `focused=None`, a point 6 px along each adjacent edge from the corner
   resolves to the corner name, not the edge band.
8. `test_geometry.py` — the same geometry built with `focused="crop"`: a
   point 6 px inside the west edge resolves to `west`, a point 20 px inside
   resolves to `crop`.
9. `test_geometry.py` — a crop 30 px tall in widget space: its centre
   resolves to `crop`, a point 6 px above its top edge resolves to `north`.
10. `test_crop_canvas.py` — at a 20x10 canvas, `content_rect` matches the
    pixmap size and offset, mirroring the existing test at
    `tests/ui/test_crop_canvas.py:131-141`.

`test_geometry.py:133`, `:153` and `:167` stay green under the new priority
and the shrunken `"crop"` region: the shrunken region is at least 24 px or
falls back to the full rectangle, and `:167` clicks the centre of a 60x60
crop and the dragged corner, neither of which the shrink or the reorder
touches.

## Implementation order

1. `core/geometry.py`: named constants, `MediaTransform.inset` with the clamp
   and the `0.0` default, inset-aware `scale`, plumbed through
   `CropGeometryState`.
2. `core/geometry.py`: full-length bands through `_effective_target`.
3. `ui/crop_canvas.py`: pass `_MEDIA_INSET` to the geometry; after
   `_rebuild_geometry`, set the layout contents margins from
   `self._geometry.transform.inset` (the clamped value), then call
   `_update_pixmap`. Order in `resizeEvent`: geometry first, margins second,
   pixmap third.
4. Update the three existing UI tests (one press point, two constants); add
   the new tests.
5. `uv run python scripts/check_guardrails.py --update`, naming the reason and
   the new figure in the commit body.

Sequential — every step touches the geometry or depends on it.
